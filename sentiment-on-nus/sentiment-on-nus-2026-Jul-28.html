1. NUS nursing leader Assoc Prof Zhou Wentao wins Singapore's top nursing honour

President Tharman Shanmugaratnam and Health Minister Ong Ye Kung conferred the President's Award for Nurses 2026 — Singapore's highest nursing accolade — on Associate Professor Zhou Wentao of the Alice Lee Centre for Nursing Studies at NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine on 27 Jul. Over nearly three decades she restructured NUS's Master of Nursing programme and, in 2025, established Singapore's first Doctor of Nursing Practice programme, while helping set a national training standard for Advanced Practice Nurses across all eight public hospitals. She has also served as Treasurer of the International Council of Nurses since 2022, carrying Singapore's advanced-practice-nursing model onto the world stage. The award places an NUS-trained and NUS-based nurse leader at the centre of Singapore's most visible nursing recognition of the year.

Sources: NUS News, NUS Medicine

2. "They really walk the talk": NUS student's cancer journey highlights the university's support system

The Straits Times' "More Than Grades" series profiled Joshua Lee, an NUS psychology undergraduate diagnosed with Stage 3 testicular cancer at 24 in 2024, midway through his degree. Rather than penalise him for missing a year-long community-leadership project, a professor arranged a replacement within two days, and staff sent gift baskets, checked in regularly and offered to help fundraise for his treatment. Lee completed his studies with honours in November 2025 — the same month he was declared cancer-free — and now works at the Singapore Cancer Society, crediting the ordeal with redirecting him toward healthcare and giving him "the courage to live a bit more unapologetically." The piece, published 27 Jul, reads as a warm, human-scale endorsement of NUS's pastoral care rather than an institutional press release.

Sources: The Straits Times

3. University of Exeter signs family-medicine pact with NUS and National University Health System

The University of Exeter announced a Memorandum of Understanding with NUS and the National University Health System on 27 Jul, establishing a long-term framework for joint research, staff and student exchanges on healthy ageing, multimorbidity, digital innovation, preventive care and population health. The deal builds on existing ties through Associate Professor Sarah Bailey, who holds posts at both Exeter and NUS, and pairs Exeter's Academic Primary Care research strength with NUS Medicine's integrated family-medicine model delivered through NUHS. Exeter's Professor Richard Follett and NUS Medicine's Professor Jose M. Valderas both framed it as the start of a broader strategic relationship rather than a one-off exchange. It's a modest but steady signal of NUS's continuing pull as a partner for UK research universities in primary-care science.

Sources: University of Exeter News

4. NUS engineering students take hotel-check-in and golf-coaching concepts to Stanford, built with Meta and Venture Corp

NUS College of Design and Engineering students completed their final showcase at Stanford University under the Stanford Global Engineering Design Innovation (GEDI) programme, presenting two industry-sponsored prototypes: PrimeDrive, an AI-assisted hotel arrival experience developed for Venture Corporation, and Golf Hawks, smart-glasses-based golf coaching built around a brief from Meta. Both projects started as capstone work under NUS's Innovation & Design Programme before evolving through a second semester of cross-border collaboration with Stanford students, testing prototypes on real users including a professional golfer. The NUS–Stanford exchange runs on a US$1.5 million gift from the Khetan Foundation to NUS Enterprise, and NUS Senior Vice President Dr Tan Sian Wee described it as a deliberate stage in "how we build founder capability at NUS." The story positions NUS's engineering pipeline as increasingly entrepreneurial and internationally networked.

Sources: EurekAlert!, NUS CDE
