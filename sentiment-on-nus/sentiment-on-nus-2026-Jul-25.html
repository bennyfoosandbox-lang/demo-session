1. Singapore opens S$130m national RNA science hub at NUS Kent Ridge

The National Initiative for RNA Biology and its Applications (NIRBA) officially opened its new 2,000 sqm Hub at NUS's Kent Ridge campus, officiated by National Research Foundation chairman Heng Swee Keat and attended by NUS President Prof Tan Eng Chye and A*STAR CEO Beh Kian Teik. Launched in 2025 as a S$130 million national initiative, NIRBA brings together NUS, Nanyang Technological University and A*STAR researchers around four research clusters spanning RNA-based cancer and disease biology, immunity, cell delivery and drug clearance. The Hub, based at Block S9, positions NUS as the anchor institution in Singapore's push to become a global hub for RNA-based medicine, diagnostics and biomanufacturing, aligned with the national RIE2030 research plan. It's a significant reputational and funding vote of confidence in NUS's biomedical research capacity from the highest levels of Singapore's government.

Sources: NUS News, BioSpectrum Asia, NIRBA

2. NUS tops Southeast Asia in mathematics, adding an eighth global top-10 subject

NUS placed 32nd worldwide (tying Imperial College London) in the newly released 2026–2027 U.S. News Best Global Universities mathematics ranking, making it Southeast Asia's highest-ranked institution in the subject — NTU was the only other Singapore university to make the list, at 234th. The mathematics result extends the same US News subject-ranking cycle covered in yesterday's digest (where NUS's computer science ranking slipped to 6th): NUS in fact placed in the global top 10 across eight subjects overall, including 1st in optics, 3rd in chemistry, 5th in artificial intelligence and materials science, and 8th in engineering and electrical/electronic engineering, while holding 2nd in Asia and 16th worldwide overall. Coming right after the CS-ranking story broke local coverage of a "slip," this fuller picture reads more positively, underscoring NUS's broad-based research strength beyond any single subject dip.

Sources: VnExpress International

3. NUS engineers build a campus-wide sensor network to predict Singapore's flash floods

Professor Simone Fatichi of the NUS College of Design and Engineering has spent 22 months instrumenting an 8.6-hectare stretch of the Kent Ridge campus — from the Central Library to NUS Business School — with meteorological stations, soil moisture sensors, groundwater wells and drain pressure transducers, logging data every five minutes to model how tropical rainfall becomes urban runoff. The team found that during major storms, soil in parks and forests can become nearly as saturated (and flood-prone) as paved surfaces, though heavily vegetated areas buffer the effect by depleting soil water storage during dry spells. It's framed as first-of-its-kind tropical fieldwork addressing a real gap in flood science, with the explicit goal of eventually forecasting flood-risk hotspots across Singapore as extreme rainfall intensifies with climate change — a concrete, locally relevant example of NUS research aimed at national infrastructure resilience.

Sources: NUS News

4. Actor Chew Chor Meng's daughter's NUS graduation draws warm local media coverage

Chloe Chew, 24, daughter of veteran Singaporean actor Chew Chor Meng, graduated from NUS with a Communications degree from the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences at this year's Commencement, prompting a celebratory Instagram post from her mother that was picked up by The Straits Times, Mothership and 8 Days. While a soft human-interest story rather than an institutional development, it's a reminder of how NUS commencement season continues to generate warm, low-key public visibility for the university through local celebrity and family narratives rather than through NUS's own messaging.

Sources: The Straits Times, Mothership, 8 Days

5. NUS hosts 1,000 educators debating how universities should teach in the AI era

NUS University Town hosted the Higher Education Research and Development Society of Australasia (HERDSA) 2026 conference from 6–9 July — the event's first time outside Australia in five years — drawing roughly 1,000 participants and marking NUS's debut as host, jointly organised with five other Singapore universities. A headline panel on 9 July, moderated by NUS's Dr Misty So-Sum Wai-Cook, brought together scholars from NUS, the University of Queensland and the University of Auckland to debate how to keep higher education "human-centric" as AI reshapes learning and entry-level job markets, with panellists urging educators to redesign assessment for AI-augmented students rather than simply policing AI use. The event functions as a soft-power win for NUS — positioning it as a regional convener of higher-education policy debate — even as the underlying discussion reflects real anxiety among Asia-Pacific educators about AI's disruption of how students learn and are evaluated.

Sources: NUS News
