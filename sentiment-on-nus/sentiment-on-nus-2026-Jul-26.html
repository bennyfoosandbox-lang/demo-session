1. NUS's 67th Rag & Flag tradition hits the streets, timed to the university's 120th and Singapore's SG60

NUS Students' Union kicked off its annual Rag & Flag campaign this week under the theme "Connecting Beyond Horizons," with Faculty Flag Day held on 25 July and Hostel Flag Day set for 1 August across roughly 120 collection points islandwide. The 1958-founded tradition — the university's largest student-led orientation event — sends freshmen out to raise funds for Community Chest-backed social service agencies before a float-and-performance showcase at Rag Day on 8 August, where Education Minister Desmond Lee is guest of honour. Organisers are framing this year's run as a tribute to NUS's 120th anniversary and Singapore's 60th year of independence, adding institutional milestone messaging to what is usually pitched as a charity and freshman-bonding exercise. Last year's edition drew over 1,500 students and raised close to $260,000; final totals for 2026 won't be confirmed until after the 1 August close, but the scale and civic framing already mark it as a visible positive-sentiment touchpoint for the university this week.

2. Former UN Under-Secretary-General Dr Noeleen Heyzer appointed NUS Pro-Chancellor

NUS Chancellor and President Tharman Shanmugaratnam has appointed Dr Noeleen Heyzer, an NUS alumna and former UN Under-Secretary-General, as Pro-Chancellor for a three-year term starting 1 August 2026. Heyzer led the UN's ESCAP from 2007 to 2014 and headed UNIFEM before that, was the UN Secretary-General's Special Envoy on Myanmar, and currently serves as Rector of NUS's Ridge View Residential College; she received an NUS honorary doctorate in 2025. She joins the university's four other Pro-Chancellors — Gautam Banerjee, Theresa Foo, S Jayakumar and Po'ad Mattar — in a largely ceremonial but symbolically weighty role that stands in for the Chancellor at Commencement and other university functions. NUS President Tan Eng Chye framed the appointment as bringing "global vision" to the university's leadership bench, positioning it as a reputational win that trades on Heyzer's international stature rather than any operational shake-up.

Sources: NUS Newsroom

3. NUS engineers pack 25,000-times-smaller radio switches onto 5G/6G chips, publish in Nature

A team led by Professor Mario Lanza at the NUS College of Design and Engineering has built memristive radio-frequency switches — made from a hexagonal boron nitride layer just 8 nanometres thick sandwiched between gold electrodes — that hold their on/off state without continuous power, unlike the transistor- or diode-based switches used in most commercial chips today. Each switch measures only 2 by 2 micrometres, about 25,000 times smaller than the conventional switches it could replace, addressing a real bottleneck: 5G radio front-ends need 50-plus such switches versus roughly four in 2G, and 6G will need more still. Built directly onto existing gallium-nitride chip platforms and tested up to 100 gigahertz with signal loss as low as 0.3 decibels, the switches survived two weeks of storage and 24 hours at 175°C without degrading, and were wired into working attenuator, power-divider and filter circuits — not just standalone lab devices. The work, published in Nature on 8 July and picked up by trade press in the past few days, is an engineering-prestige story that reinforces NUS's positioning in next-generation telecoms hardware, though the team flags two remaining hurdles — manufacturing-compatible integration and higher cycle-life — before commercial use.

Sources: RF Globalnet, NUS College of Design and Engineering, EurekAlert

5. NUS Medicine-backed smartwatch pilot at National University Hospital moves to wider rollout

National University Hospital (NUHS), working with researchers from the NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine, is expanding a smartwatch-based vital-signs monitoring programme after a month-long pilot completed in June found the wearables flagged deteriorating patients up to two hours earlier than routine manual nursing checks. Thirty inpatients wore Health Sciences Authority-approved smartwatches continuously tracking blood pressure, pulse and blood-oxygen saturation, with nurses still performing standard bedside verification before any escalation — the system is explicitly designed as a clinical decision-support aid rather than an autonomous monitor. NUH now aims to equip roughly a tenth of suitable inpatients with the devices within 12 months, a concrete scale-up of an applied research collaboration that ties NUS's medical school directly to a tangible patient-safety improvement rather than a purely academic output. Coverage of the expansion has circulated through health-industry trade wires over the past ten days.

Sources: Healthcare IT News, BioSpectrum Asia, PR Newswire
