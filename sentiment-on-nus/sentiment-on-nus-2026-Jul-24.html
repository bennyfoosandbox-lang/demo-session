1. NUS slips to 6th in US News computer science rankings — but climbs four spots overall

The 2026–2027 U.S. News Best Global Universities Subject Rankings, released in June and still driving coverage this week, show NUS falling to 6th worldwide for computer science, down from 2nd the previous year, while crosstown rival NTU held steady at 4th. Singapore remains the only Southeast Asian nation with two universities in the global CS top 10. The subject-level slide contrasts with NUS's overall trajectory: in the same U.S. News rankings, NUS actually climbed four places, from 20th to 16th globally, and its optics programme topped the world rankings outright. 2. NUS School of Public Health launches regional BRIDGE network with WHO partners across six Southeast Asian nations

NUS's Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health convened representatives from Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Singapore, Thailand and the Philippines this week to establish the Data Capacity and Systems' Working Group under its new BRIDGE (Bridging Regional Information and Data for Governance and Health Equity) initiative. Global partners from the WHO Regional Office, WHO Europe and the ACPHEED Secretariat Establishment Office joined the working sessions, which are organised around three pillars: health system performance assessment, data capacity and systems, and global burden-of-disease modelling. The initiative, backed by the Precision Public Health Asia Society and the CMB Foundation, positions NUS as a regional convening hub for health-data policy work across ASEAN — a reputational win that reinforces the university's public-health research profile beyond Singapore's borders.

Sources: NUS Saw Swee Hock School of Public Health

3. NUS mathematicians unveil AI method that predicts material behaviour from microscopic data

A team led by Associate Professor Qianxiao Li in the NUS Department of Mathematics has developed AI methods that learn the large-scale behaviour of complex materials directly from microscopic observations, sidestepping the need for prohibitively expensive atom-by-atom simulations. The work — validated on systems including simulated alloys with over 500,000 atoms — was published across two venues: Physical Review Materials and the 2026 International Conference on Machine Learning. By automatically identifying a small number of hidden variables that capture collective system behaviour, the approach could accelerate materials discovery for energy, electronics and manufacturing applications. The dual peer-reviewed and top-tier-conference placement signals strong external validation of NUS's applied-AI research and adds to the university's case for research-reputation metrics even as headline rankings fluctuate.

Sources: NUS Faculty of Science, Tech Xplore

4. NUS Medicine undergraduates take top research prizes for liver-disease and eye-inflammation studies

NUS Medicine announced its 2026 Undergraduate Research Opportunities Programme winners this week: See Rui Ze Aedann received the Distinguished Undergraduate Research Prize for a 374-patient multinational registry study questioning whether current diagnostic criteria adequately capture real-world tubercular uveitis cases, while N Apoorva Sasikumar, Glenn Ho Jun Kit and Nicole Tang Shu Ying won the Outstanding Undergraduate Research Prize for a network meta-analysis of 64 trials on emerging MASH (fatty liver disease) therapies. Both projects have already been published in international peer-reviewed journals — Investigative Ophthalmology & Visual Science and Cell Press's Med — and presented at overseas conferences including ARVO 2025 and the World Ophthalmology Congress. The awards showcase NUS's undergraduate research pipeline producing clinically relevant, internationally published work well before graduation, a point of pride the university is actively publicising.

Sources: NUS Medicine

5. WongPartnership endows S$1 million gift, launches Alvin Yeo Visiting Professorship at NUS Law

Singapore law firm WongPartnership announced a S$1,000,000 endowed gift to NUS on 22 July, establishing the Alvin Yeo WongPartnership Visiting Professorship at the Faculty of Law in memory of its late Senior Partner and co-founder, who died in 2022. Administered through NUS Law's Centre for Asian Legal Studies, the professorship will bring a distinguished commercial-law and dispute-resolution scholar or practitioner to NUS annually starting in the 2026/2027 academic year to teach, research and deliver a public lecture. NUS Law Dean Professor Andrew Simester called it recognition of WongPartnership's support since a 2009 scholarship endowment. The gift is a notable philanthropic vote of confidence in NUS Law from one of Singapore's largest law firms, and continues a pattern of major private-sector gifts to NUS this year.

Sources: WongPartnership LLP
