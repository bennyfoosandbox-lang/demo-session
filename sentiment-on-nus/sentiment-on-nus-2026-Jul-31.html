1. Singapore's PM hosts Malaysia's deputy king as the 55th UM–NUS golf tournament renews a 58-year-old tradition

The 55th UM–NUS Inter-University Tunku Chancellor Golf Tournament brought Universiti Malaya's Chancellor and Malaysia's Deputy Yang di-Pertuan Agong, Sultan Nazrin Shah of Perak, to Singapore from 29 to 30 July, continuing a fixture first played in 1968 to cement ties between NUS and its sister institution across the Causeway. The visit doubled as a diplomatic occasion: Prime Minister Lawrence Wong hosted Sultan Nazrin for breakfast at the Istana on 30 July, where the two discussed deepening ASEAN cooperation and a range of joint Singapore–Malaysia initiatives, with Wong calling the countries "bound by a shared history and strong people-to-people ties." For NUS, the biennial tournament — alternating between Malaysia and Singapore — keeps the university's oldest continuous inter-institutional relationship visible at the highest levels of both governments, well beyond campus circles.

2. NUHS's new pharmacy AI platform is projected to save 850 pharmacist-hours a week

The National University Health System has begun rolling out NCAIP (NUHS Cluster AI in Pharmacy), a bundle of four AI tools — Admission/Discharge MedRecon, MedTriage and MedVerify — that automate medication reconciliation, patient triage and order verification across its hospitals and polyclinics. Built on NUHS's ENDEAVOUR data platform, the tools hit strong early accuracy in validation studies: MedRecon topped 88–90% accuracy, MedTriage was 100% accurate at flagging patients needing in-person counselling, and MedVerify exceeded 95% on medication-order review. NUHS estimates that once all four tools are fully deployed, the platform could free up to 850 pharmacist-hours a week and roughly S$2 million a year, redirecting staff toward complex cases such as heart failure and precision medicine. The project, built by a team of 43 people including pharmacists and developers over six months and nearly 1,600 reviewed cases, is now being pitched to other Singapore public health institutions for a possible nationwide rollout.

Sources: Healthcare IT News

4. Former NUS President Tan Chorh Chuan retires from Singapore's top science-and-research post

Professor Tan Chorh Chuan — who led NUS as its second president from 2008 to 2017, a tenure that saw the founding of Yale-NUS College — will retire from the Administrative Service on 1 September after more than four decades in public service, most recently as Permanent Secretary for National Research & Development and the PMO's Science & Technology Policy and Plans Office. Coordinating Minister Chan Chun Sing thanked Tan for contributions that "strengthen our capabilities in healthcare, higher education, research and innovation," crediting him with steering the RIE2030 research-funding roadmap and Singapore's National AI R&D plan. Tan, an NUS medicine graduate who led Singapore's public health response to the 2003 SARS epidemic as Director of Medical Services, will stay on as non-executive Chairman of A*STAR. DSTA chief Ng Chad-Son, 51, succeeds him as Permanent Secretary, taking over both the research portfolio and a concurrent Defence Development appointment at MINDEF.

Sources: Public Service Division, AsiaOne

5. NUS chemist co-leads major Frontiers in Science review on lanthanide-based medical imaging

A review published in Frontiers in Science on 30 July, senior-authored by Prof Xiaogang Liu of NUS with first author Dr Yuxia Liu, maps how "lanthanide carriers" — engineered materials built around rare-earth ions such as europium, terbium and gadolinium — could drive the next generation of biomedical imaging and "theranostics," approaches that combine diagnosis with treatment. The review surveys progress across optical, X-ray and MRI imaging and argues lanthanide-based probes, paired with AI-assisted image analysis and smarter drug-delivery systems, could sharpen tumour detection, vascular imaging and disease-progression monitoring, though most applications remain preclinical. Liu's team frames the work as bridging materials science, biophysics and clinical need toward more personalised diagnostics; co-author Dr Jorge Méndez-Ramos of Spain's University of La Laguna calls lanthanides the "vitamins of industry," with largely untapped biomedical potential. The paper adds to NUS's steady run of internationally published materials-science and imaging research this year.

Sources: News-Medical, Frontiers
