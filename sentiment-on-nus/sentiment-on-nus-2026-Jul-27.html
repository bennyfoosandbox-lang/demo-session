1. Shanmugam tells NUS Law freshmen that lawyer-attrition concerns are real, but so is the pressure that "comes with the territory"

Speaking at NUS Law's freshmen inauguration ceremony on 26 July, Coordinating Minister for National Security K Shanmugam addressed a recent Law Society study that flagged long hours, toxic workplace culture and bullying as drivers of lawyer attrition. He said the attrition rate has stayed "broadly stable" over the past decade — roughly 60% of lawyers remain in practice after five years and 40% after ten — even as the profession grew 26% and in-house counsel ranks swelled to about 4,800. Shanmugam told the incoming cohort that top-tier legal careers pay well (first-year associates at leading firms can clear S$100,000) but demand round-the-clock intensity, while stressing that none of that "excuses poor supervision... harassment, bullying and unreasonable treatment." The address, delivered at NUS's University Cultural Centre, was carried by three separate national outlets, keeping NUS Law's flagship freshman ceremony inside the wider debate over Singapore's legal-sector working conditions.

Sources: CNA, The Straits Times, The Business Times

2. NUS Law alumnus Justice Sushil Nair to become Singapore's 5th Chief Justice — Faculty keeps the celebration going a week on

The Prime Minister's Office announced on 17 July that Justice Sushil Sukumaran Nair — an NUS Bachelor of Laws graduate, Class of '89 — will succeed Sundaresh Menon as Singapore's 5th Chief Justice when Menon retires on 26 February 2027. It's an older announcement than this digest usually covers, but NUS Faculty of Law has kept it alive all week, publicly congratulating its "distinguished alumnus" across Facebook and LinkedIn, and the story kept generating fresh pickup: the Philippines' Chief Justice met with the incoming Singapore CJ on 24 July, and legal trade press linked the appointment to Singapore hosting the IBA's 2027 annual conference. Nair joined the Bench in 2025 after 35 years at Drew & Napier leading its restructuring practice, and was elevated to the Court of Appeal on 15 June 2026. For NUS, it's one of the highest judicial offices in the country going to one of its own law graduates — a reputational marker the Faculty is still actively amplifying.

Sources: Prime Minister's Office, NUS Faculty of Law, Mothership

4. On World Mangrove Day, NUS spotlights a geographer teaching students to see mangroves as "climate infrastructure"

To mark World Mangrove Day on 26 July, NUS Newsroom profiled Dr Gretchen Coffman, a Senior Lecturer in the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences whose "roots to reefs" approach treats mangroves, seagrass meadows and coral reefs as one connected system rather than isolated habitats. Coffman noted Singapore has lost the vast majority of its historical mangrove cover but still hosts 35 true mangrove species — comparable to Indonesia's 48 — and pointed to Sungei Buloh and Pulau Ubin as evidence the country has become a regional conservation leader. She described mangroves as "natural climate infrastructure" that stores carbon, buffers storm surges and supports fisheries, and detailed how she takes NUS students into the field across Singapore, Indonesia and Malaysia — including drone-based 3D mapping — to design real mangrove-restoration proposals. The piece frames NUS environmental education around hands-on, region-spanning fieldwork rather than classroom theory alone.

Sources: NUS Newsroom

5. NUS Computing pair's 2022 healthcare-IT paper wins a top Chinese Ministry of Education research award

NUS Computing announced on 27 July that a 2022 paper co-authored by Provost's Chair Professor Teo Hock Hai and Professor Tan Chuan Hoo, both from the Department of Information Systems and Analytics, has won the Youth Achievement Award at China's 10th Ministry of Education Award for Outstanding Scientific Research Achievements — one of the country's top national honours in humanities and social sciences, selected from 206 winning papers nationwide. The paper, "Rural-Urban Healthcare Access Inequality Challenge: Transformative Roles of Information Technology," published in MIS Quarterly, examined how health IT can narrow China's rural-urban care gap, drawing on fieldwork that documented patient frustration occasionally spilling into protests against under-resourced rural clinics. It's the second honour for the research, which previously won the Chinese Economic Society's Information Management Research Award in 2024. For NUS, it's a concrete, if modest, data point of Singapore-based information-systems research being recognised at the highest levels of Chinese academia.

Sources: NUS Computing
